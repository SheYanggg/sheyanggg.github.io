About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Materials Science and Engineering at Beijing Institute of Technology, advised by Prof. Xinghua Yu.

I study how heat and stress develop during welding using finite-element simulation and physics-informed machine learning. My current work covers wire arc additive manufacturing, parallel thermal simulation, and in-process monitoring.

Research projects

  • Doctoral research · September 2024–present

    Physics-informed thermal modeling for wire arc additive manufacturing

    Developed physics-informed PyTorch models for transient temperature prediction in single-track wire arc deposition and evaluated them against finite-element reference solutions.

    Read project

  • Study lead · First author

    Magnetic-field-assisted TIG welding

    Designed the methodology and curated the data for a study of how field direction affects arc deflection, weld bead geometry, and grain size near the fusion line.

    Read project

  • Software contributor · Second author

    Multimodal defect detection in gas metal arc welding

    Contributed software to MBGN, a model that combines synchronized welding current, voltage, and molten-pool imagery. It achieved 0.719 macro-F1 on the held-out evaluation set.

    Read project

  • Benchmark contributor · Co-author

    Parallel finite-element simulation of welding heat transfer

    Contributed to FEniCSx/PETSc benchmarks of three-dimensional transient welding simulations. The team evaluated 17 solver and preconditioner combinations on 1–16 CPU cores.

    Read project
