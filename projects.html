Doctoral research · September 2024–present

Physics-informed thermal modeling for wire arc additive manufacturing

The temperature field in wire arc deposition changes throughout the pass. This project studies how physics-informed models can predict that transient field for a single track.

My contribution

I developed the models in PyTorch and used finite-element reference solutions to benchmark the temperature predictions.

Research focus

The comparison grounds the machine-learning predictions in the same thermal problem as the numerical reference. This work forms part of my doctoral research on welding thermomechanics.

Magnetic-field-assisted TIG welding

This study investigated how the direction of a static magnetic field influences arc deflection, weld bead geometry, and microstructure during TIG welding of Q235 steel.

My contribution

I led study design and data curation and wrote the first draft of the article. The study compared weld-bead measurements with microstructural evidence from electron backscatter diffraction.

Findings

Changing field direction affected where the arc deflected and how the bead formed. Among the orientations tested, the study reported a fusion-line grain size of 3.47 µm under the X-directed field and 3.99 µm under the Y-directed field.

Software contributor · Second author

Multimodal defect detection in gas metal arc welding

MBGN uses signals collected during welding to identify defects. It combines synchronized current and voltage waveforms with high-speed molten-pool images.

My contribution

I contributed software to the project. The model fuses waveform and visual features in a bidirectional feature pyramid and applies a learned gate before classification.

Evaluation

On the held-out evaluation set, MBGN reached 0.748 accuracy and 0.719 macro-F1. Its macro-F1 was 0.120 higher than that of the strongest task-matched multimodal baseline reported in the paper.

Benchmark contributor · Co-author

Parallel finite-element simulation of welding heat transfer

Large transient thermal simulations spend a substantial share of their compute time solving sparse linear systems. This work compared solver and preconditioner configurations using FEniCSx and PETSc.

My contribution

I contributed to multicore performance evaluation and solver benchmarking for three-dimensional transient welding simulations.

Benchmark scope

The team compared 17 solver and preconditioner combinations on 1–16 CPU cores while holding the physical model, discretization, and solver controls fixed. The thermal model was checked against published 304 stainless steel welding data.
